Accountability Rationales

Select a decision to expand or collapse its rationale.
Budget Allocation
Accountable: Chief Financial Officer
The CFO is accountable because investment allocation is an enterprise capital decision requiring tradeoffs across initiatives and business priorities. This role can weigh expected value, operating cost, risk exposure, and competing funding demands across the transformation portfolio.
Architecture Standards
Accountable: Chief Technology Officer
The CTO owns the target architecture and therefore has final authority over enterprise standards and justified exceptions. Central accountability reduces incompatible technology choices that could weaken platform reuse, scalability, security, or modernization.
Data Governance
Accountable: Chief Data Officer
The CDO owns enterprise expectations for data quality, ownership, classification, access, retention, and acceptable use. Technology, security, and business leaders execute those policies, but the CDO provides one enterprise authority for cross-business data decisions.
Initiative Prioritization
Accountable: Chief Product Officer
The Chief Product Officer balances customer value, strategic alignment, operational need, and constrained delivery capacity. A single portfolio owner prevents initiatives from competing independently for the same funding and engineering resources.
Vendor and Partnership Decisions
Accountable: Chief Information Officer
The CIO is accountable because major external technology relationships affect enterprise operations, cost, resilience, security, and long-term platform direction. Technical, security, data, and commercial leaders contribute due diligence before the decision is made.
Eno Scale and Channel Expansion
Accountable: Eno Product Lead
The Eno Product Lead owns recurring decisions about adoption, service performance, channel readiness, operating support, and risk evidence. This makes expansion a continuing product-governance decision rather than a one-time launch approval.
Slingshot Internal vs. External Priorities
Accountable: Slingshot General Manager
The Slingshot GM owns tradeoffs between external customer commitments and Capital One's internal platform needs. A single commercial owner is answerable for product performance, customer obligations, and escalation when shared resources cannot satisfy both priorities.
